# Changchun and Harbin Bao Mahjong

Both rooms play eight hands. The winner deals next, and a draw keeps the dealer. A player declares
ready by choosing a discard that leaves a valid wait. The announced rack stays private. Both rooms
honor the first winner in turn order. A declared player only discards the drawn tile unless a
Changchun kong extension preserves the entire wait.

## Changchun Shared Bao

[Pook's Changchun rules](https://www.pook.com/games/ccmj.html) describe the 136-tile wall, egg kongs,
private shared bao and payment schedule. Chows come from the preceding seat. The hand uses all three
numbered suits, a terminal or honor, and at least one pung or kong. Dragon eyes exempt the pung
requirement. Four declared sets may win with a lone tile waiting for the eyes when all sets are
pungs or kongs. An unannounced natural hand can win.

Wind eggs use East, South, West and North. Dragon eggs use White, Green and Red. Ones and nines
eggs use the same rank across all three suits. Each egg takes tiles from the opening rack. One
Bamboo can fill a missing face. Such a One Bamboo contributes to neither the Bamboo suit nor
terminal qualification. Each egg occupies one set. Four-tile eggs take a replacement and three-tile
eggs take none. Initial egg and extension payments are one per opponent. Further
tiles of that family and further One Bamboos can extend the egg. An extension is offered to the
opponents for robbery or pon and is scored only if it completes. The dealer cannot extend a pong
or egg before the first discard. Regular open kongs pay one from each opponent and concealed kongs
pay two. Kongs of One Bamboo, One Dot or dragons double those payments. Kong payments survive a
draw. The reserve starts at 14 and grows by two for each completed kong after the first.

On the next draw of a ready player, select one of the twelve reserved tiles as the shared bao.
Only that player sees the new bao. Other ready players see it at the start of their own next draw.
Three publicly visible copies retire the current face. Choose another previously unused reserved
position at the next ready draw, with the old tile still in its original position. The wall owns
each physical indicator throughout. A match with a waiting face offers a win before the draw.
A physical self-drawn tile matching the seen bao can fill any declared wait. Discards use their
printed face.

Start with one fan. Add one each for self-draw, a closed hand, a qualified one-tile edge, middle or
pair wait, and a physical bao draw. Matching a newly seen bao adds two and skips the self-draw
addition. All pungs add two. Each opponent pays 2^(fan − 1), doubled for a bill involving the
dealer and doubled for a discard or robbed-kong supplier. Declaring a win on a revealed reserve
bao includes that face in the winning display. A physical wild draw retains its actual displayed face.

## Harbin Four Bao Slots

Pook's guides cover the [112-tile wall](https://mm.pook.com/hebmj/rule/rule-1.html), [ready and winning
conditions](https://mm.pook.com/hebmj/rule/rule-3.html), [bao visibility](https://mm.pook.com/hebmj/rule/rule-4.html),
and [winning types](https://mm.pook.com/hebmj/rule/rule-5.html). Use three suits and four Red Dragons.
Ready requires at least one open set, at least two numbered suits, and a terminal or Red Dragon.
Four declared sets cannot go ready. A qualifying completion includes a chow and a pung or kong.
Red Dragon eyes exempt the pung requirement. A wait on two pairs can supply the final pung.

A ready chow or ready pon may claim from any seat if a legal discard can immediately announce
ready. These claims come after wins, before ordinary calls, with ties going to the closest seat in
turn order. The caller must confirm a ready discard. A ready player can claim a natural win or
robbery and takes no further calls or kongs.

Four physical tiles stay at the tail. Reveal a slot in order each time another player goes ready.
Only ready players see the revealed slots. They all see the same active slots and each owns the slot
from their declaration. A self-drawn copy of an active face fills any declared wait. The own-slot
award wins ties if both own and another slot have that face. A sole middle wait earns the middle
award, with an extra bonus when the drawn bao is that exact waiting face. The four tiles stay fixed.

The Pook Harbin pages identify the winning types and their relative strength. Dojo uses the following
payment schedule. A discard win costs the supplier 1 and a robbed added kong costs them 2. Ordinary
self-draw costs each opponent 2. Drawing an own bao costs each 4 and drawing another player's bao
costs each 2. A sole middle wait doubles those shares, and a bao matching that very wait doubles them
again. A direct kong costs its supplier 1, an added kong costs each opponent 1 and a concealed kong
costs each opponent 2. Immediate kong payments survive a draw.
