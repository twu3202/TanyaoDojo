# Jiaxing Raise the Head and Pinghu Flowers and Faan

Each room has four seats and runs for eight hands. One selected ordinary tile is displayed and removed. The other copies of that face act as wildcards in concealed winning shapes. White Dragons keep their printed face unless selected. Wildcards may be discarded, and no player may take them from a discard or include them in called sets.

## Jiaxing Raise the Head

The room follows the [Game Tea House Jiaxing rules](https://wap.gametea.com/ask/201809/7367.html). It uses 136 tiles with no flowers. It plays to the last tile. A standard four-set-and-pair hand qualifies as All Pungs, Pure Suit, Mixed Suit or All Honors. A player can win by self-draw or rob an added kong. A wildcard hand that can qualify on any structurally available next draw cannot rob a kong. The check accounts for the displayed tile and the player's own melds and hand.

All Pungs adds 2 tai. Pure Suit, Mixed Suit and All Honors add 4, 2 and 10 respectively. A Dragon set adds 1, a seat-wind set adds 1 and a round-wind set adds 1. A single wind set can receive both the seat and round bonuses. A hand with no wildcards adds 1 tai. Each faan doubles the tai total. Three wildcards, a kong replacement win, a last draw, and a genuine single wait after four declared sets each add one faan. A qualifying opening draw for the dealer or a nondealer adds two. The dealer stays after winning or drawing, otherwise the deal moves to the next seat. The round wind advances after each four dealer changes.

A draw, flipping an honor, rolling a pair of matching dice, or repeating the previous flipped face each adds a carried bonus. The first available bonus is used at the next win and adds one faan. Any others remain available. Dice and the flipped tile use independent seeded draws.

Three calls on tiles supplied by the same opponent link the two players. Chow, pon and direct kong count as feeds. An added kong retains its original feed. Unlinked active seats choose whether to stay in play. A player who folds automatically passes on claims, discards each new draw, and is exempt from payment. Their discards remain available for ordinary calls. Folding closes their eligibility to form a new liability link for that hand. If later calls create another link, unlinked active seats can decide again.

When a linked player wins, their partners pay five shares each and other active opponents pay two. When an unlinked player wins, linked seats pay two and other active opponents pay one. A self-draw without links charges every opponent one. A robbed added kong without links charges only its supplier three. With links, add three shares to the supplier's applicable normal bill. Thus a linked supplier can pay eight shares. The closest accepting winner takes the hand.

## Pinghu Flowers and Faan

The [Bianfeng Pinghu score table](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/510.html) gives the flower and faan awards. The [Game Tea House Pinghu play guide](https://www.gametea.com/ask/201809/7357.html) describes replacements, ordinary discard wins, stealing an added kong, and the eight-stack reserve. This room selects a four-player, 144-tile table with a fixed 16-tile reserve. It uses a flipped face as the wildcard, no score cap, and the optional dice, wind wildcard and three-feed multipliers switched off.

The eight flowers are revealed and replaced automatically, including when another flower comes from the tail. The winning hand pays `(1 + flower count) × 2^faan`. Each exposed bonus flower adds one flower. A wind pung adds one, a wind open or added kong adds two, and a wind concealed kong adds three. Other open or added kongs add one and other concealed kongs add two. A declared or naturally held dragon quartet and each complete named flower quartet are scored as Four Flowers, one faan for each. A flower or kong replacement win and a robbed added kong each trigger the same one-faan award. The score also counts All Pungs, No Wildcards, Three Wildcards and Single Wait at one each, Mixed Suit at one and Pure Suit at two. An actual wildcard can complete a concealed set for scoring, with literal Dragon quartets evaluated on the printed tiles.

A discard pays the closest accepting winner one share from the supplier. Self-draw pays one share from each opponent. A robbed added kong makes its supplier pay three shares. These payment amounts and the shared displayed-wildcard convention are room choices where the score table omits details. The winner deals next, or the dealer stays on a draw.
