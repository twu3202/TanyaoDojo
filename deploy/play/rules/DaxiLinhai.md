# Daxi and Linhai mahjong

Both rooms play eight hands with four seats and the standard 136 tiles. Their scoring, win bonuses and dealer changes follow their own room rules.

## Daxi · Buy the Base

[Zhejiang Game Hall’s Daxi rules](https://www.hzbianqu.com/article/1123.html) specify a 16-tile draw reserve, overlapping discard wins, a buy-in and three separate kong bills. This room uses the ordinary 1-point win and disables the optional bounce and double-self-draw modes. It uses ordinary four-set-and-a-pair hands with natural tiles.

The dealer buys automatically. Before tiles are shown, each nondealer can buy or pass. All seats see the choices once the hand starts. A discard win charges its supplier 1 point. Self-draw charges each of the other three seats 1 point. Each applicable payment gains one point if the winner bought and one if its payer bought. Buy-in points move only when someone wins. For example, if three nondealers pass and the dealer self-draws, each pays 2. If a player who bought wins on a discard from another player who bought, that supplier pays 3. Multiple winners are paid independently.

A direct kong charges its supplier 1 point. An added kong charges the original pon supplier 1. A concealed kong charges each opponent 1. A successful robbery of an added kong takes the offered tile and settles as a discard win. A canceled kong earns no payment. Kong points are posted immediately and retained on a draw. Kong replenishment uses the live tile closest to the reserve boundary. After chow or pon, a player discards before they can declare a kong on a later draw.

Passing on a discard win or passing on the available pon locks that same face for the corresponding action until the player draws. The dealer continues if among the winners, including multiple winners. Otherwise the next seat clockwise in engine turn order takes the deal.

## Linhai · Six Chong Tiles

[Zhejiang Game Hall’s Linhai rules](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/847.html) describe White Dragon wildcards, up to three tai, six chong tiles and reciprocal liability after three feeds. The room selects six chong tiles with a fixed 14-tile reserve. A wildcard can represent any face in a concealed winning shape and cannot be taken from a discard or used in an exposed set. A passed discard win locks all further discard wins until the player draws. Several players can accept the same discard or robbed added kong.

A set of the winner's seat wind or a dragon adds one tai per set. A mixed suit adds one tai and a pure suit adds three. Three wind sets with the fourth wind as the pair add three tai for Four Winds. The room uses the highest tai across complete wildcard assignments and caps it at three. The starting share of one doubles per tai. After a win, reveal the next six tiles from the wall's draw end, including unused reserve tiles if necessary. Multiple winners score the same revealed tiles independently. Each matching tile adds one to that seat's share beyond the eight-point base cap.

| Winner's seat wind | Matching ranks in all three suits | Matching honors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| East | 1, 5, 9 | East |
| South | 2, 6 | South, Red Dragon |
| West | 3, 7 | West, Green Dragon |
| North | 4, 8 | North, White Dragon |

Only the supplier pays on a discard win or robbed added kong. On self-draw, each opponent pays the winner's full share. Three chow or pon feeds from the same seat create a public, reciprocal link. A linked seat covers all three shares on the partner's self-draw, using the earliest established link when there are several. An added kong preserves the pon that formed the link. For example, two tai and three hits give a seven-point share. A linked self-draw then charges the partner 21. A discard win still charges the supplier seven.

An added kong can be robbed. Kong replacement rolls two dice and uses their sum minus two as the zero-based offset from the live-wall boundary beside the reserve, wrapping within the remaining live tiles. A draw makes no transfer. If the dealer wins they stay, otherwise the first accepting winner takes the deal. On a draw, the deal advances one seat. These dice, simultaneous-win and claim conventions resolve details left open by the published room description.
