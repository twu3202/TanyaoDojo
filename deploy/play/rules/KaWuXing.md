# Three-player Ka Wu Xing

These rooms use the operator descriptions for [Suizhou](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/1249.html)
and [Xiangyang](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/1205.html). The
[local game guide](https://kawuxingmajiang.zocet.com/) supplies the two-winner dealer rule
used by the Suizhou room. Values below also record the conventions chosen for details the pages leave open.

## Shared play

- Three seats and 84 tiles. Use dots, bamboo, and the three dragons. The first dealer is random.
  The dealer receives 14 tiles and the other two receive 13. Pon and kong are allowed. Chows are not.
  A completed ordinary hand or seven pairs may win from a discard or by self-draw.
- A discard that leaves tenpai can be declared **open ready**. The table reveals the resulting concealed hand.
  Later draws remain private until discarded or used to win. Open-ready seats can win or discard their draw;
  this profile closes all other calls for those seats. Legal declarations use structural waits and exclude
  a fifth copy in the declaring player's hand and melds.
- A direct kong bills the supplier 2. An added kong bills each opponent 1, and a concealed kong bills each 2.
  These are the room's fixed kong values. A later kong has its own payment; replacement draws come from the wall tail.
  An added kong needs the newly drawn fourth tile, so choosing pon over an available direct kong forfeits the upgrade.
  No kong is legal after the last draw. Pon and wins may still be taken on the last discard.
  A robbed kong earns no points and moves only its winning tile.
- At wall exhaustion the first seat to go open ready pays each other seat 1. Kong payments stand, and the
  dealer repeats. A single winner deals next after a win.

## Hand values and liability

- Use one base point and multiply compatible patterns. Regular hands are ×1, all triplets ×2, pure suit ×4,
  middle-five wait ×4, small or big three dragons ×4 or ×8, and four exposed sets with a concealed pair ×4.
  Choose the highest-valued legal decomposition. A middle-five wait requires the winning tile to complete
  a 4–5–6 sequence in a valid decomposition.
- Seven pairs is ×4, doubled for every concealed quad, including the three-quad extension. It can stack with
  pure suit. A concealed four-in-one is ×4 and an exposed four-in-one is ×2. Those patterns require use of the
  winning tile unless the hand is pure suit in Suizhou, or has another special base shape in Xiangyang.
  Only unmelded quads or an unmelded tile matching a pon score this pattern. Seven pairs scores its quad through
  the pairs multiplier alone.
- Open ready, robbing a kong, a replacement-draw win, and a win on the last live draw each double the hand.
  Suizhou also doubles a win on the immediate post-kong discard, once per winning event.
- A self-draw bills both opponents. An ordinary discard or robbery bills only the supplier. On a
  discard to an open-ready seat, the supplier pays the equivalent of both self-draw shares.

| Rule | Suizhou | Xiangyang |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hand cap | Uncapped | Eight per share before liability |
| Both seats open ready | Supplier still pays two shares | Supplier pays one share |
| Two wins offered | Both may accept, supplier deals next | Nearest accepting seat wins and deals next |
| Robbing a kong | Added and concealed | Added only |
| Passed win | Only a higher offered score can win before the next draw | No further offered win until the next draw |

The Xiangyang page lists compatible patterns and a cap without a complete multiplier table.
This room uses the shared base values above for those patterns. The lobby identifies each room and links
the corresponding operator rules.
