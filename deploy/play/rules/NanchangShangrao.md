# Nanchang and Shangrao Mahjong

Both rooms use 136 tiles, four players and eight hands. The numbered faces wrap within a suit.
Winds cycle East–South–West–North, and dragons cycle Red–Green–White.

## Nanchang Double Jing

[Golden Island Nanchang](https://www.80166.com/guide/rule/ncmj.html) describes honor chows, two
upper jing, separate lower jing, the 17-stack reserve, and distinct winning and reward schedules.
The Dojo room uses one closest winner on a discard. Opponents may chow from their upper seat.
Any three distinct winds or the three dragons can be a chow, whether declared or concealed.
A declared set uses printed faces. An upper jing can complete concealed sets, pairs, or a separated
hand. A discarded jing can be claimed at face value and belongs to its new holder for scoring.

The two public flips are from the same reserved stack. The upper face and its successor are
primary and secondary jokers. The lower face and its successor are used only for an opening
reward calculation, as described in [Tongcheng's lower-jing explanation](https://m.tcy365.com/news/d26024.html).
Both indicators remain physically in the 34-tile reserve. A kong replacement comes from the live
edge adjacent to that reserve. All kongs show four faces. The dealer cannot kong in the first action.
Regular kongs charge 2 from each opponent. A secondary-jing kong charges 10. Four matching first
discards charge the dealer 5 per opponent in this room.

Upper and lower jing rewards use 2 per primary and 1 per secondary held or claimed. Lower rewards
use the opening racks. Upper rewards use final racks and melds, both on wins and on draws. Each table
receives a separate zero-sum ledger. The only owner of jing doubles that ledger's reward. At 5 raw
points the reward doubles, at 6 it triples, and each further point increases that multiplier by one.
If a claimed upper jing crossed that threshold and the receiver kept at least 5 points thereafter,
the supplier covers the sum of the three extra threshold payments. Ordinary jing credit keeps its
three separate bills. This is the Dojo interpretation of the operator's threshold and sponsor rules.
The room follows the Golden Island rule that a held jing is immediately usable and that a discarded
jing counts for its claimant. It does not require a special piao-jing declaration.

A standard win has four sets and a pair. Seven pairs and fourteen separated tiles score separately.
Numbered tiles in each suit of a separated hand must be at least three ranks apart and honors cannot
repeat. Seven-star separated needs all seven actual honor faces, including when one is also jing.
A standard, pairs, or separated win possible with all jing at their printed faces can earn German.
Double German is determined at settlement if no player holds upper jing. Jing eyes is a self-draw
on a pre-draw hand with a jing left as the eyes and complete natural sets alongside it. German
and jing eyes are alternate awards in this room.

The win base is 1. Seven pairs and ordinary separated double it, seven-star separated quadruples
it, and self-draw or a kong replacement multiplies by 2 or 4 respectively. With the corresponding
opening condition, a heavenly or earthly win uses the source's 20 multiplier instead of the normal
draw multiplier. German or jing eyes doubles the base and adds 5 per bill. Double German doubles
the German base again. Dealer involvement, the supplier of a discard and the supplier of a robbed
kong apply their published factors to the multiplicative portion. The 5-point addition follows
those factors. All opponents pay. Passed wins remain blocked until the seat regains a turn or an
intervening call skips its turn, unless the observable winning bill increases. Double German uses
opponents' racks only at settlement and is excluded from that decision.

## Shangrao Bao Self-Draw

[Tongcheng's self-draw profile](https://m.tcy365.com/news/d8362.html) specifies numbered chows,
the successor bao, and no discard wins. The four physical copies of the successor face can substitute
in a winning rack. All calls use printed faces. The reserve holds 14 tiles in this room, and its
indicator is selected by a visible single die. Robbing a kong is unavailable. The dealer stays on a
draw and otherwise the winner deals next.

[The operator's hand list](https://m.tcy365.com/news/d22296.html) describes all pungs, seven pairs,
fourteen separated tiles, terminal and honor hands, and bao eyes. A single bao left with a rack
of complete sets can match any next draw to form the eyes. This includes four previously declared
sets. A terminal-and-honor award needs a qualifying shape made within those faces, with the bao
substituting if needed.

This Dojo score table uses 1 for a standard hand. Seven pairs, all pungs, and separated hands each
earn one fan. A printed four of a kind in seven pairs gives the luxury version at two fan. A valid
terminal-and-honor shape adds one fan, or two when closed, and a closed kong adds a further luxury
fan to the latter. Bao eyes and a kong replacement each earn one fan. The three opponents pay the same
bill. Direct/added/closed kongs each charge every opponent 1/1/2, kept on a draw. The published
self-draw profile supplies the win rules. The numerical schedule, reserve, and printed kong formation
are fixed Dojo conventions.
