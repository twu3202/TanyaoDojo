# Taizhou four-player mahjong

This room follows [Zhejiang Game Hall’s four-player rules](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/936.html). It uses the standard 136 tiles and one winner per hand. The dealer opens with 14 tiles. The next remaining tile is turned up and set aside. Other copies of that printed face are wild in concealed winning shapes. White Dragons play at the flipped face’s natural value. Wildcards may be discarded and cannot be claimed or used in calls. A player who passes on a win or a pon cannot take the same face for that action until they draw or call. The dealer stays after winning. Another winner or a draw moves the deal one seat. The room runs for eight hands.

## Hand values

| Set | Ranks 2–8 | Terminals and honors |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Open pung | 2 | 4 |
| Concealed pung | 4 | 8 |
| Open or added kong | 8 | 16 |
| Concealed kong | 16 | 32 |

The seat-wind pair or any dragon pair adds 2 hu. The winner starts at 10 hu and adds 2 hu each for all pungs, an inside wait, and self-draw. A pung finished by a claimed tile takes the open value. A seat-wind pung or kong, a Red Dragon set, and a Green Dragon set each add one faan. For the winner, a pure suit adds three faan, a mixed suit adds one, and having no wildcards or using every wildcard as its own printed face adds one. Each faan doubles the sum and the resulting hand value is capped at 100 hu. The winner's highest raw score across legal shapes and winning-tile positions determines the score and its breakdown.

Each opponent adds points for their own declared sets, concealed groups of three matching natural tiles, and remaining valued pairs. They also score the seat-wind, red and green set faan. Wildcards in these hands keep their printed faces for this count. A robbed added-kong tile belongs to the winner for settlement. The all-wildcards-restored convention is also described by [Game Tea House’s Jiaojiang scoring guide](https://www.gametea.com/news/201809/7432.html).

## Pairwise settlement

Compare the values of each pair of seats. With both below 100, the lower hand pays the difference, halved if both seats are nondealers. The room keeps half-point amounts. A 100-hu hand receives the full 100 from each uncapped hand. Equal values transfer zero. For example, with the dealer's value 34 and the other values 18, 41, and 7, the dealer pays the 41-hu hand 7. The 18-hu hand pays the dealer 16 and the 41-hu hand 11.5. The 7-hu hand pays 27, 5.5, and 17 to the three higher hands. The settlement dialog shows all payments and the four net score changes.

## Liability and wall conventions

The room uses a fixed 16-tile reserve and enters the fresh-tile phase with 30 tiles total, choosing the even ends of the published 16/17 and 30/31 ranges. The first discard of a face counts as fresh. It becomes familiar even if someone calls it. A fresh discard that supplies a win in this phase makes the discarder liable. A winner with a pure suit and at least three chows from their upstream seat makes that seat liable on self-draw, or makes the supplier liable on a discard or robbed added kong.

Before the late phase, a fresh printed dragon that completes the winner's matching dragon role makes the supplier liable if their hand is exactly one draw and discard away from structural tenpai. The same applies to a fresh White Dragon used at a suited flipped face if the supplier holds a singleton Red or Green Dragon. This room's structural check counts the supplier's concealed and melded copies and the flipped tile, and ignores availability among the other hands and wall. These dragon cases also apply in the late phase, where the general fresh-discard rule already assigns the same payer.

Set the liable player's value to zero, calculate the ordinary pair amounts, and assign all outgoing bills to that player. A hand that draws at the reserve has no scoring or transfers. The room uses the ordinary fresh-tile and liability rules, and the standard 2-hu value for all pungs.
