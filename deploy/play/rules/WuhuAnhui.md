# Wuhu Eight Suit Tiles and Anhui Red Dragon

These Dojo profiles use four seats, eight hands and ordinary points. The player deals first. The next winner
deals after a win. Wuhu keeps the current dealer after a draw. Anhui uses the last player to draw.

## Wuhu

Wuhu has two separately documented profiles. The [four-wind rules](https://wuhumj.uc55.com/Rule/Detail/201803_000279.html)
include each wind once. The [515 rules](https://mobile.xinyueyouxi.com/game/237-20?from_app=0&region=&time=1440730121)
use the four natural Red Dragons in their place. This changes which honors can complete a pair, pon, or kong.

- Both use 112 tiles. Pon and kong are allowed. Chow, seven pairs and robbing a kong are disabled in these rooms.
  A hand needs four sets and a pair, with at least eight physical tiles in one numbered suit. Each kong adds four
  tiles to that count. The first accepting player in order after the discarder wins.
- A unique inside wait means the winning face completes the middle of a sequence and no other face can complete
  a legal hand with eight suit tiles. A face with all its copies already in the player's own tiles is excluded
  from this test. Other seats' exposed tiles do not change the wait pattern.
- The ordinary awards are all pons, a pure straight, a face in both a triplet and a sequence, a mixed one-suit
  hand with honors, and a win on a kong replacement. The fourth copy in a pair does not earn the root award.
  A previously called pon can contribute to an award. Four tiles of a declared kong do not qualify for it.
- A pure suit, a winning opening draw, two root faces, or two kongs in another suit alongside eight tiles in
  one suit qualify as exceptional. Three ordinary awards also cross that threshold.

| Component | Four winds | Red Dragon 515 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base, in order of discard, inside discard, self-draw, inside self-draw | 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 | 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 |
| Suit value | Largest suit count plus middle rank if applicable. Halve on a discard and round down. | Same, with the self-draw doubling applied before rounding. |
| Ordinary award | 20 each. Double on self-draw except kong replacement. | 5 each. Double on self-draw. |
| Winning kongs | Add 2 exposed or 4 concealed. | Same. |
| Exceptional score | 100 replaces the ordinary components. | 50 replaces the ordinary components, plus 20 per ordinary award and another 20 on self-draw. |
| Dealer | Add the base to each bill that involves the dealer. | Same. |

The four-wind discard supplier pays once. If one opponent fed three pons to the winner, that opponent pays
instead. An upgraded pon still counts as a pon. On self-draw each opponent pays independently. The 515 table
charges all three opponents for either kind of win. The operator publishes more than one Wuhu schedule.
This room uses its 515 schedule in a points match with no buy-in or separate cash ledger.

## Anhui Red Dragon

The [operator’s Anhui rules](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/783.html) specify red-dragon calls, natural-only
robbery, four-red wins and the supported reserve sizes. The Dojo profile uses 108 numbered tiles and four
wild Red Dragons. Red Dragons can be discarded and cannot be called or declared as a kong. An added kong
uses the drawn fourth tile. Ordinary discards cannot be won on. A player with any Red Dragon in hand cannot
rob an added kong. With four Red Dragons, a player can win on their own draw without a standard shape.

This room uses the published four-horse option. Its reserve stays separate from ordinary draws and kong
replacements. On a win, read the four reserved tiles together. If any horse count is short, repeat the final
available tile for scoring only. Each 1, 5, 9 or Red Dragon adds 2 to a 2-point win share. A self-draw charges
each other seat its share and the supplier of a robbed added kong pays three shares. As a room convention,
the first player in turn order who accepts the robbery wins. The family’s standard 3-from-supplier direct
kong, 1-from-each added kong and 2-from-each concealed kong payments apply. Those transfers stand on a draw.
The table uses four sets and a pair with no seven pairs and gives no independent closed-hand or natural-hand bonus.
