# Jinyun Classic and Cantonese Style

Both rooms use four seats and 136 tiles. The [operator’s Jinyun rule page](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/61.html) lists separate tables for Classic, Huzhen and Cantonese Style. The playable Classic and Cantonese rooms each run eight hands.

## Classic

The flipped face is wild, with the other three copies available. White Dragons play as the flipped face. A wildcard can complete a concealed hand and may be discarded, where it cannot be claimed. The wall stops at 33 tiles, the first total below the published 34-tile threshold. Kong replacements draw from the live end beside this reserve. Ordinary discard wins and robbed added kongs have one winner, chosen in turn order. The winner deals next. On a draw the dealer stays unless a nondealer completed a kong, in which case the deal advances one seat.

A plain win takes 2 per share on self-draw and 1 on discard. Pure Suit, Seven Pairs, Single Wait after four sets, All Pungs and Thirteen Orphans each count 4 on self-draw and 2 on discard. Compatible specials can add across the same winning interpretation. A dealer opening win, a win on the dealer’s first discard, a self-draw after a kong, and a final live self-draw each add 4 as applicable. This room recognizes Wild Gathering when a valid self-drawn shape contains all three available wildcards. All Honors is its own 40-point award. The [Android rules](https://www.hzbianqu.com/game/234.html) give an additive Wild Gathering and Seven Pairs example. This room uses the main operator table’s 4-point opening discard win and leaves the optional hard pairs and Seven Honors awards off.

All three opponents pay on self-draw, discard and robbed added kong. Robbing an added kong uses the self-draw values. A winner-to-payer bill is doubled if it includes the dealer. Every opponent pays a completed open or added kong 1, or a concealed kong 2. Kong transfers stay on a draw.

If the dealer and next two players each discard the same opening tile without an intervening call, the fourth player’s opening discard determines the payment. Matching the other three makes the dealer pay 2 to each opponent. Discarding a wildcard instead makes the dealer pay 1 to each. Any other discard makes the fourth seat pay 1 to each. This room applies the rule to the first circuit and retains the transfer even if the hand later draws. The result shows this payment separately.

## Cantonese Style

This room uses natural tiles, pon and kong. The reserve is nine tiles, the first total below the published ten-tile threshold. Ordinary wins are by self-draw. On a win immediately after a direct kong replacement, the seat that supplied the kong covers all three shares. A robbed added kong makes the player who attempted the kong pay all three shares. The first accepting seat wins. Other self-draws collect one share from each opponent.

The share is the highest matching award in the normal point table. A plain hand pays 1. All Pungs and Seven Pairs pay 2. Pure Suit, All Honors, Single Wait after four sets and Four-of-a-Kind Seven Pairs pay 4. A dealer opening win pays 20 and Thirteen Orphans pays 30. The room uses an ordinary dealer change to the winner and retains the dealer on a draw.

A direct kong immediately charges its supplier 3. An added kong charges each opponent 1 and a concealed kong charges each opponent 2. These charges are independent of any win. A successful robbery earns no kong points for the canceled kong. When no one wins, all kong charges are reversed and the result shows the refunds.
